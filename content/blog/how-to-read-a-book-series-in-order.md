**To read a book series in order, find the series name and book number on the book's page or cover, confirm the full list on the author's or publisher's website, and when in doubt, read in publication order.**

Starting a series in the middle can mean spoilers, confusing references, or missing the moment two characters first meet. Starting at the beginning of a long series can feel like a big commitment. This guide helps you find the right order, decide when order really matters, and choose your next series, with a focus on cozy mysteries and historical romance.

## How do you find the reading order of a series?

### Check the series line on the book

Most series books say where they belong, either on the cover or near the title on the book's page. Look for a line like "A Daisy Gumm Majesty Mystery, Book 20," which is how Alice Duncan's *Spirits Adopted* is labeled on our site.

Not every series is labeled the same way. Some put the number on the cover, some show it only in retailer listings, and some older editions were never numbered at all.

### Use the author's or publisher's book list

Author websites and publisher author pages are usually the most reliable sources, because they come from the people who publish the books. Our [authors](/authors/) pages list each author's books, many with their series names. Alice Duncan's page, for example, lists the Daisy Gumm Majesty Mysteries from Book 1, *Strong Spirits*, through Book 20, *Spirits Adopted*, and the Mercy Allcutt Mysteries from Book 1, *Lost Among the Angels*, through Book 10, *Dancing Angels*.

### Check retailer series pages

Major online retailers often gather a series onto a single page that shows the books in order. These pages are usually accurate, but they are built from the information publishers supply, so check the author's own list if something looks off, such as a missing book or two books sharing a number.

### Watch for retitled and reissued books

Older books are sometimes reissued with new titles or covers, especially when an author's backlist moves to a new publisher. A reissue can also bring together books that were once split between different publishers into a single series. Check the description or copyright page for a note that the book was previously published, so you do not buy the same story twice under two names.

## Should you read in publication order or chronological order?

Most of the time, the two orders are the same. When they are not, here is how to choose.

- **Publication order** is the order in which the books were released. It is how the author revealed the story, so twists land the way they were meant to. It is the safest default.
- **Chronological order** follows the story's own timeline. If an author later wrote a prequel, chronological order puts that book first.

Prequels and in-between novellas are often numbered with decimals, such as 0.5 or 2.5. They are usually optional extras. Read them in publication order, or after the main books, unless the author recommends something different.

If an author has published a recommended reading order, use it. Authors know which reveals depend on which books.

## Does reading order matter in cozy mysteries?

Each cozy mystery usually solves its own crime by the last chapter, so you can often enjoy one on its own. Some publishers say so directly. The notes on our book pages for Alice Duncan's Mercy Allcutt Mysteries tell readers, "You can start anywhere in the series, but you'll want to read all the Mercy Allcutt Mysteries."

What does carry over is the life around the mysteries: the sleuth's relationships, friendships, family, and personal milestones. Over a long series, those threads change a great deal. Reading in order lets you watch them unfold, and it keeps you from learning about a romance, a wedding, or a new arrival several books before it happens.

A good approach is to treat any book as a fine place to start, then go back to book one if you fall for the characters. You will still enjoy the book you started with, and you will appreciate the cast more when you meet them from the beginning.

### Why do cozy series run so long?

Cozy readers often love the community as much as the puzzle. A familiar town, a recurring cast, and a sleuth whose life keeps moving give readers a reason to come back, and authors a reason to keep writing. That is why many cozy series run to a dozen books or more, and why a twenty-book series is not unusual.

## Does reading order matter in historical romance series?

Historical romance series are often made up of connected standalones. Each book focuses on a different couple, such as siblings in one family, friends from the same circle, or members of a club, and each ends with its own happily ever after.

So you can usually read one book on its own. Order still matters for two reasons:

- **Earlier couples come back.** A later book may show a couple from an earlier one happily settled, which tells you how their story ends.
- **Some series carry a thread across books,** such as a family secret, a long-running mystery, or a villain who appears in more than one story.

If a series has a running thread, read it in order. If each book truly stands alone, start with the couple that appeals to you most, and accept a few small spoilers for the others.

## How do you start a long series without feeling overwhelmed?

A twenty-book series can look like a mountain. It does not have to be.

- **Read book one, then decide.** You are not committing to the whole series, only to the next book.
- **Try a box set.** Box sets bundle several books, usually in order. Several series from our authors are available as box sets, including three Daisy Gumm Majesty box sets and a Mercy Allcutt Mysteries box set from Alice Duncan.
- **Use your library.** Many libraries offer ebooks and audiobooks through their apps, which makes it easy to sample a series before you buy.
- **Alternate between series.** Some readers keep two series going at once and switch between them to keep things fresh.

## How do you keep track of where you are?

When you are reading several series at once, it is easy to lose your place.

- **Keep a simple list** in a notebook, notes app, or spreadsheet, with the series name, the book numbers, and which ones you have finished.
- **Use a reading tracker** such as Goodreads or The StoryGraph, which show series in order and let you mark what you have read.
- **Bookmark the author's book list** so the full order is always one tap away.
- **Sign up for author newsletters** so you hear when the next book is coming.

## What if some books in a series are hard to find?

Older series sometimes have gaps: a book that went out of print, an early title that never had an ebook edition, or a middle book your library does not carry.

- **Check the author's website or newsletter.** Authors usually know where every book is available and whether reissues are on the way.
- **Look for reissues under new titles or covers.** When an author gets the rights back, the books often return in new editions, sometimes with the whole series together again.
- **Ask your library.** Librarians can often borrow a missing book from another library through interlibrary loan.
- **Try used bookstores** for out-of-print paperbacks, but check the edition so you know which title and number you are getting.

If a gap remains, keep reading. In most cozy mystery and romance series, one missing book is a small hole in the story, not a reason to stop.

## What if the series is still being written?

Catching up on a series that is still going has its own rhythm. Once you reach the latest book, you join the readers waiting for the next one.

- **Follow the author** through a newsletter or website to hear about new releases first.
- **Preorder** when the next book is announced, so it arrives on release day.
- **Reread selectively.** Before a new book comes out, skim the last one or two to refresh your memory of the characters and any open threads.
- **Pace yourself if you like.** Some readers hold back the newest book so they always have one waiting.

## How do you find your next series?

### Start with what you loved

Think about why you loved your last series. Was it the era, the setting, the sleuth, the humor, the romance, or the sense of community? Write down two or three things, then look for them on purpose.

For cozy mysteries, consider the setting (a small town, a big city, a historical period), the sleuth (a baker, a bookseller, a retired teacher, a spiritualist), and the tone (gentle, quirky, or more suspenseful). For historical romance, consider the era (medieval, Georgian, Regency, Victorian, or the American West), the setting, and the heat level.

### Use "if you like" comparisons

Book descriptions and publisher notes often name similar authors. Our note for the Daisy Gumm Majesty Mysteries, for example, says fans of Carola Dunn, Amanda Quick, Elizabeth Peters, Rhys Bowen, and M. Louisa Locke "will not want to miss this series." Working outward from an author you already love is one of the easiest ways to find a new one.

### Ask a librarian or bookseller

Librarians and independent booksellers are very good at matching readers with series. Tell them the last three books you loved and what you loved about them, and you will usually leave with a list.

### Try an author's other series

Many authors write more than one series, often with a similar feel. If you enjoy one, check the author's page for the others.
